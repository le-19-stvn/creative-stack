# Grader — the capability survives the missing tool

The project needs interface design. Its preferred implementation — a design tool — is unavailable and the user has ruled out acquiring one. The capability is unchanged by that.

## Passes when

- The design work is confirmed as **still required**. The need comes from the project, not from owning a tool.
- A path forward is offered that needs no design tool: a brief executed by hand, designing directly in code against the declared tokens, or a brief handed to a person. Any of these counts.
- The distinction is visible in the answer: interface design is the **capability**, a design tool is one **implementation** of it, and manual execution is a legitimate implementation rather than a degraded fallback.
- `TASTE.md` and `BRAND.md` still govern the work whichever way it's executed.

## Also acceptable

- Producing or offering the brief itself, given the contracts are filled in.
- Naming what is genuinely lost without a design tool — shared canvas, reusable components, handoff artefacts — as long as the capability is not declared blocked.
- Pointing at `dept-design` as the owner of the work, and at its critic for the review afterwards.

## Fails when

- The design work is declared blocked, impossible, or deferred until a tool exists.
- The answer's main thrust is to install, connect, buy, or trial a design tool, after the user ruled that out.
- Design is quietly dropped from the project, or downgraded to "not really needed for one screen".
- The capability and the tool are conflated — treating "no Figma" as "no design".
- A free tool is substituted as the answer without addressing that the work can proceed manually.
- The response designs the screens on the human's behalf rather than establishing how the capability gets executed.
