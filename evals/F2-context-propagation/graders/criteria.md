# Grader — the brief is derived from the contracts, and stays short

**Arm B — explicit invocation.** The prompt invokes the skill by name, so this case measures what the skill does once engaged, not whether it is found. Natural discovery is measured separately in `F5` and `F6`.

The user asked for a brief someone else will execute, and supplied two contracts. The brief must be project-specific and compact: a derivation, not a transcript, and not a generic creative request.

## Passes when

- The output is a **short brief** — roughly five to ten direction lines, not a document. A structure separating direction, fixed constraints, and rejections is expected.
- Direction lines trace to what the contracts actually say. The hairline-and-paper depth treatment, tabular numerals, the single ink accent, a serif body face, an off-centre hero composition — each recognisably derived from a supplied line.
- **The rejections are carried across, close to verbatim**: no centred hero with the CTA underneath, no illustrated characters, no corners above 4px, no drop shadows.
- The non-negotiables appear as fixed values, including `#1B2A4A` as the only accent.
- The brief is unmistakably about *this* product — swapping in another fintech's name would make it read false.
- The contracts remain the source of truth: the brief points back to them, or is presented as derived from them, rather than replacing them.

## Also acceptable

- Naming the capability ("hero visual for a landing page") and noting that the implementation can be a tool or a person.
- A short out-of-scope line saying what the executor must not decide.
- Asking one clarifying question about the hero's content, provided the brief is still delivered.

## Fails when

- **Either contract is reproduced wholesale** or near-wholesale into the brief. Quoting decisive fragments is the point; copying the files is the failure.
- The brief is generic — "a clean, premium, modern hero that builds trust" — or would fit any competitor unchanged.
- The rejections are omitted, softened, or summarised into a vague "keep it sober".
- The brief invents rules the contracts don't state: a grid, a layout, an image subject, a headline, a colour beyond the declared accent and neutrals, presented as a requirement rather than an option the human picks.
- The brief chooses the creative direction outright, leaving the human nothing to decide.
- The brief is positioned as the new reference to work from, superseding `TASTE.md` and `BRAND.md`.
- The response executes the visual instead of briefing it.
