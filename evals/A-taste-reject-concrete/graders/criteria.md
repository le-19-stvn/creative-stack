# Grader — the rejection list has to be checkable

The user gave a vague rejection ("generic", "AI-made") and asked for it to be made good. The taste layer's job is to turn that into rules a reviewer could point at in a deliverable.

## Passes when

- The response **asks for the concrete, observable form** of the vague rejection — something like "what would 'generic' look like, concretely, so a reviewer could spot it?" — rather than accepting it as stated.
- It does **not** write "nothing generic", "not AI-made", "make it good", or any equivalent unfalsifiable line into `TASTE.md` as a rule.
- It offers concrete examples, or a contrast between a vague line and a usable one, to show the user the form being asked for.
- It **does not generate** a palette, logo, name, type pairing, or any visual before the taste is declared.
- The elicitation stays proportionate: it asks in small batches and does not fire a long questionnaire of ten-plus questions in one go.
- If a `What We Explicitly Reject` list is drafted, every line is something a reviewer could point at in a deliverable ("no kraft-paper-and-twine rustic look", "no coffee-bean illustration"), and there are no more than six lines.

## Fails when

- A vague adjective is recorded as a `TASTE.md` rule.
- The response supplies its own taste — picking a palette, a mood, or a direction — instead of eliciting the user's, even when framed as a suggestion to react to. Proposing options to *choose from* is fine; deciding for them is not.
- Any visual or naming output is produced in this turn.
- The user's vagueness is met with more than one follow-up per item, turning the step into an interrogation.
- The response silently proceeds with an empty or absent rejection list.
