# X / Twitter — channel notes

Read under the `social-post` law: this file governs **form only**. The claim, audience, enemy, proof and voice come from `POSITIONING.md` unchanged.

## Rules first

- **No engagement bait.** Reply-farming questions, "bookmark this", "RT if", quote-tweet dunks aimed at a competitor's account.
- **No fabricated stat.** A number with no source travels fast here and is checked fast. Every figure traces to `POSITIONING.md` proof.
- **Attack the habit, not the account.** The enemy is a status quo; naming a company to start a pile-on isn't a position, it's a fight.

## What the form rewards (durable mechanics)

- **Compression is the craft.** One sentence that carries a full idea beats three that circle it. Cut the setup — this channel is hostile to warm-up.
- **The first post must stand alone.** It gets read, quoted and screenshotted without the rest. If it needs the thread to make sense, it's not finished.
- **Quotable over complete.** A claim someone can repeat verbatim in their own words travels; a summary doesn't.
- **A thread is one argument decomposed**, and every post in it has to earn its slot. If a post only exists to keep the thread going, delete it.
- **The reply is a real format.** Adding the brand's specific knowledge to someone else's thread often lands better than posting into the void.

## Anti-patterns — reject on sight

- "A thread 🧵" as the value proposition, or a numbered thread of generic advice.
- "Let that sink in." / "Nobody talks about this." / "Here's why that matters:" as filler beats.
- The hook that never lands its claim.
- Reposting the LinkedIn version with the line breaks removed.

## Where the position shows

Brevity tempts you to drop the proof, and that's exactly the failure this channel produces. If the claim doesn't fit with its evidence attached, the post is a thread — not a shorter claim with the evidence quietly removed.
