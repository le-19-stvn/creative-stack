# LinkedIn — channel notes

Read under the `social-post` law: this file governs **form only**. The claim, audience, enemy, proof and voice come from `POSITIONING.md` unchanged.

## Rules first

- **No engagement bait.** "Agree?", "comment YES", "repost if you…" — the algorithm's voice, not the brand's.
- **Never invent a personal story.** No fabricated client, failure, or conversation to manufacture credibility. If the brand hasn't lived it, it isn't written.
- **Don't post a competitor's name into a pile-on.** The enemy in `POSITIONING.md` is a status quo or a habit — attack that, not a company's staff.

## What the form rewards (durable mechanics)

- **A first line that stands alone.** The feed truncates. The opening must carry a complete, specific idea *before* the cut — not a teaser that withholds it.
- **One idea per post.** A second idea halves both. Split it into two posts, or drop one.
- **Short paragraphs, one thought each.** Whitespace is legibility on this feed, not decoration.
- **Specificity over polish.** A number, a named situation, a real constraint beats a well-turned abstraction every time.
- **A close that asks for something real** — a question the brand actually wants answered, or the one next step. Not a ritual CTA.

## Anti-patterns — reject on sight

- **Broetry**: one-line paragraphs building to a manufactured lesson.
- The humbling anecdote as a device ("I turned down my biggest client. Here's what it taught me about focus.").
- "Unpopular opinion:" followed by a widely held opinion.
- Hustle framing, emoji bullets standing in for structure, a hook that never lands its claim.

## Where the position shows

This feed rewards a stated stance, and `POSITIONING.md` already names one — the enemy. Use that. Don't invent a spicier take for the feed; a borrowed controversy fails the swap test the moment the critic reads it.
